# Stamps

Open `index.html`. Drop, paste, or choose an image and it becomes a postage stamp: a rectangle with semicircular perforations cut along all four edges. The main download is a white proof sheet with the stamp above the untouched original; a secondary action saves the stamp alone as a transparent PNG. No dependencies or build step.

- **Image:** click the source card, drop a file anywhere on the page, or paste from the clipboard. A generated dune sample is loaded on first visit and is replaced once you supply your own image.
- **Crop:** click the stamp preview or `crop image` under it to open the crop editor. It shows the full original with a crop box: drag inside the box to move it, drag a corner or edge handle to resize, and `reset` restores the full image. `apply crop` sets the stamp's photo frame to exactly that region, and the stamp's aspect ratio follows the crop. The thumbnail and preview update immediately. Loading a new image starts from the full frame.
- **Perforations:** the count is taken along the long edge; the short edge receives as many holes as fit at the same spacing. Hole size is a fraction of the closest notch spacing, capped below half so adjacent notches never overlap. Corners stay square, and the paper border keeps the notches clear of the photo.
- **Paper:** six tints from ecru to ink. A fine grain and a hairline inner edge are applied inside the silhouette; the edge line flips light on dark paper.
- **Export:** `download sheet` draws a white page with the stamp above the original photo, scaled to share the same width. `or just the stamp` exports the perforated stamp by itself at 1800 px on its long edge with a transparent background. Both are rendered fresh at export resolution rather than upscaled from the preview.

The preview renders at 1500 px on the frame's long edge and is scaled down with CSS, so it stays sharp on high-density screens. Images are decoded locally with `createImageBitmap` (with an `<img>` fallback), drawn to a canvas, and never leave the browser. Because the paper border is sized after the crop, the selected region is never cropped a second time. The sheet scales long or very tall originals so the output height stays under about 4600 px.
